Cities, Culture, and Economy: Quiz 2

Name: _____________________________

1) Ebenezer Howard is associated with the idea of the

a) tenement city

b) garden city

c) concrete jungle

d) hyper-dense city

2) Le Corbusier saw the city as

a) a kind of machine

b) a lousy place to live

c) fine the way it was

d) a spontaneous order that can’t be fully planned

3) The garden city sought to combine:

a) the best of New York and Chicago

b) the best of London and Paris

c) the best of the city and the country

d) all of the above

4) In his design of cities, Le Corbusier loved:

a) chaos

b) unplanned encounters

c) quaint old neighborhoods

d) geometry and repetition

5) Frank Lloyd Wright sought:

a) to eliminate the city

b) to build very dense cities, since that is eco-friendly

c) to make every city resemble San Francisco

d) to revive the city and increase urbanization

6) F. A. Hayek thought the most important part of knowledge for making economic choices is:

a) knowledge of peculiar places

b) scientific knowledge

c) knowledge that can easily be gathered from across the economy in one planning mind.

d) knowledge of the particular circumstances of time and place

7) Le Corbusier’s plan for workers was:

a) to turn them into parts of a machine

b) to liberate them so that their jobs were more fun

c) to rouse them to overthrow the capitalists

d) to replace them by robots

8) For Hayek, economic activity is planned. The key question is:

a) How long will it take to draw up the plans?

b) Are the plans done up in a nice Powerpoint?

c) How many steps there are in each plan?

d) Who is to do the planning?

9) According to Hayek and Oakeshott, rationalism could be summed up as:

a) saying conscious thought doesn’t matter

b) finding ratios for all objects

c) thinking all knowledge is technical knowledge

d) using your brain when you ought to just use your instincts

10) For Hayek, economic problems arise in response to:

a) steady-state conditions

b) change

c) labor unrest

d) capitalist exploitation

11) Some garden cities were actually built. Today, they are largely not cities, but:

a) piles of rubbish

b) transportation and industrial centers

c) suburbs

d) garden centers

12) For Le Corbusier, the plan should be:

a) loosely adhered to

b) the despot

c) a relatively trivial part of a great city

d) all of the above

13) According to Hayek, statistical summaries tend to:

a) hide change, because they deal in abstractions

b) highlight change, because they give us the raw numbers

c) be good reading

d) be more important than local knowledge

14) A good example of a city built with Le Corbusier’s ideas in mind is:

a) New York

b) London

c) Brasilia

d) none of the above

15) For Hayek, in a market economy, the chief way knowledge is communicated worldwide is:

a) the Internet

b) telegraph

c) prices

d) pony express

16) One characteristic of rationalist urban planning is:

a) the use of Sanskrit as the planning language of choice

b) a lack of humility

c) the use of short blocks

d) shrubbery

17) Hayek notes that the market/price system was:

a) designed by Amun Cheopulpops, an Egyptian scribe of the Twelfth Dynasty

b) never a real success

c) deliberately put in place by capitalists to exploit the workers

d) the result of human action, but not human design

18) When confronted with the failure of their rationalist urban plans, the planners tended to believe:

a) they were being too ambitious

b) they just needed a better plan

c) their style of planning itself might be at fault

d) the capitalists had sabotaged them

19) A rationalist physician, faced with a sick patient, is likely to:

a) design a new, better plan for a body

b) use chiropractic

c) attend carefully to the patient’s particular circumstances

d) none of the above

20) The marginal rate of substitution concerns:

a) how we substitute one product for another on the margin

b) the conditions for economic efficiency

c) decisions such as whether to employ more workers or more machines

d) all of the above
