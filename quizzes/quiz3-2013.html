Cities, Culture, and Economy: Quiz 3

Name: _________________________________________________

1) Ebenezer Howard wanted to build:
a) monumental cities
b) broadacre cities
c) dense cities
d) garden cities

2) Le Corbusier favored cities that are:
a) diverse
b) complex
c) neatly laid out
d) unplanned

3 ) The monumental city features:
a) massive, classically designed building spread out in a park
b) a vibrant mix of small businesses
c) suburban housing
d) farmland

4) For Jacobs, an important element of city safety is:
a) ears to the wall
b) eyes on the street
c) noses to the grindstone
d) a tongue on the road

5) Jacobs ideal city neighborhood would consist of:
a) skyscrapers in a park
b) uses carefully segregated by zoning laws
c) only families with small children
d) a mix of stores, workshops, residences, bars and more

6) According to Jacobs, am important type of person for keeping order on a city street is:
a) the vagrant
b) the storekeeper
c) the computer programmer
d) the comatose

7) Jacobs thinks that an important draw of an area in a city is:
a) the sight of other people
b) the presence of ice-cream trucks
c) vast empty spaces
d) junkyards

8) Jacobs believes that every person you meet in a city:
a) should be your BFF
b) has to be welcomed into your home
c) need not become an intimate friend
d) all of the above

9) Per Jacobs, casual friendships in a city
a) can endure for decades
b) never last
c) are BS
d) should keep their eyes on the street

10) City sidewalks are:
a) a great place to socialize children
b) the first thing Jacobs would eliminate
c) generally far too wide
d) of no use at all

11) Neighborhood parks
a) are best placed in an isolated, out-of-the-way place
b) should be as numerous as possible
c) should never contain a swimming pool
d) should be in a busy area of mixed uses

12) Jacobs thinks wiping out a thriving business to build a park
a) is always a plus
b) is not a good idea
c) will result in a thriving park
d) all of the above

13) In Jacob’s view, nice buildings, good schools and beautiful parks are:
a) nice, but the city’s functioning is what really matters
b) totally unnecessary
c) silly
d) all it takes to have a great city

14) City neighborhoods are:
a) the bones of commerce
b) the organs of self-government

a) only large businesses
b) only small businesses
c) a mix of large and small businesses
d) all of the above

16) City diversity:
a) stimulates more diversity
b) has to be minimized
c) sucks
d) has nothing to do with a lively city

17) Cities are likely to have a wide range of
a) entertainment options
b) shopping options
c) cultural facilities
d) all of the above

a) disappear
b) become huge or die
c) become more numerous
d) suffer huge losses

19) To get people on the street at different times of day, cities need:
a) superblocks
b) border vacuums
c) tons of parks
d) mixed primary uses

20) Jacob’s thinks that large civic centers filled with museums, courts, and plaza space are
a) likely to become dead zones
b) something every city needs
c) worth every penny
d) an important unifier for the city

21) City planning that sorts cities into one-use areas is
a) great for diversity
b) necessary for health
c) ensures lively neighborhoods
d) tends to create dull areas

a) be very safe, since no one is around

c) be lively

23) Jacobs likes
a) long blocks
b) short blocks
c) leggo blocks
d) wooden blocks

a) allow for more encounters between people
b) waste precious city space
c) need too much street cleaning
d) none of the above

a) to give the neighborhood that hipster feel
b) to have a sense of the past
c) where else would rats live?

26) Jacobs’ attitude to huge development projects that build a whole neighborhood at once was:
a) they should always be done if the money is available
b) they are likely to build a boring neighborhood
c) the bigger, the better
d) none of the above

a) they cause slums; they must be torn down
b) if a neighborhood is going down hill, old buildings are the symptom, not the cause

28) Jacobs thought densely populated neighborhoods:
a) would generally be awful

29) Jacobs calls neighborhoods with too little density:
a) 2-3 zones

a) everyone who lives in them is obese
b) they get huge government subsidies
c) they contain many old buildings
d) they are secluded from city life

31) In city building, maximum efficiency means:
a) standardization
b) bastardization
c) sterilization

a) to have very uniform buildings

a) they look ugly

a) it is probably the cause of the neighborhood’s decline

a) too much diversity

d) eyes on the street

a) create short blocks

a) economic vibrancy
b) multiple primary uses
c) diversity
d) low-value uses

39) Border vacuums tend to:
a) chop up a city and divide neighborhoods
b) bring people together
c) promote economic cooperation
d) all of the above

a) mixed primary uses
b) short blocks
c) a large highway run through the middle of a neighborhood
